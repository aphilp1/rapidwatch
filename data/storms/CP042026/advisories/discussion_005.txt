Tropical Storm Pena Discussion Number   5
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   CP042026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Sat Nov 07 2026
 
Visible and infrared satellite imagery show that Pena's convective 
structure has degraded slightly since the previous advisory. The 
previously well-defined center has become obscured by an expanding 
central dense overcast (CDO), with embedded cloud-top temperatures 
as cold as -80 degrees C. A 1938 UTC ASCAT-B scatterometer pass 
showed an asymmetric system with peak winds of around 40 kt located 
northeast of the center, which helped verify the initial wind radii. 
The latest subjective Dvorak classification from HFO was T3.0/45 kt, 
in good agreement with objective estimates from UW-CIMSS that range 
between 42 and 47 kt. Based on these data, the initial intensity is 
held at 45 kt.

The initial motion is estimated to be west-northwestward, or 285/8 
kt. Pena is expected to maintain a general westward motion over the 
next couple of days while steered by a broad low- to mid-level ridge 
anchored to its north. Track guidance remains tightly clustered on 
this westward heading through 72 h. Thereafter, an approaching 
upper-level shortwave trough should induce a gradual turn toward the 
northwest with a slight increase in forward speed. The updated NHC 
track forecast is largely unchanged from the previous package, 
nudged only fractionally northward in the short term, and lies close 
to the HCCA and GDMI aids.
 
Pena's intensity forecast remains a bit of a conundrum. Statistical 
SHIPS guidance continues to insist on gradual intensification 
throughout the period, responding to warm SSTs, low vertical wind 
shear, and adequate mid-level moisture. However, there remains some 
indications that Pena is likely contending with mid-level dry air 
entrainment and marginal upper-level winds, which could suppress 
significant strengthening over the next 36 to 48 h. The dynamical 
and statistical guidance reflects this tension with 
larger-than-normal model spread, yielding lower-than-usual forecast 
confidence. Consequently, the new NHC intensity forecast remains 
very similar to the previous advisory, situated near the consensus 
aids while remaining slightly higher than HCCA and GDMI throughout 
the period.
