Tropical Storm Pena Discussion Number  17
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   CP042026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Tue Nov 10 2026

Satellite imagery over the last couple of hours shows Pena's 
convective shield expanding. With how large the system's deep 
convection is, however, it is difficult to track Pena's inner vortex 
and thus fully assess the cyclone's strength. SAR microwave imagery 
from around 1630 UTC shows little organization under the convection, 
and recent visible images suggest the low-level center lies near the 
western edge of the convection. Subjective and objective Dvorak 
estimates are also relatively steady since the last advisory, with 
HFO at 2.5/35 kt and UW-CIMSS between 30-40 kt, respectively. As a 
result of these data, the initial intensity is held steady at 35 kt.
 
Pena's motion has turned west-southwest at around 8 kt. The 
cyclone's motion continues to be steered by a high pressure ridge 
anchored to storm's northwest. Track guidance has Pena continuing on 
this motion for the rest of the day, before the skillful guidance 
turns it back to the west tomorrow and then to the west-northwest 
later in the week. The latest NHC track forecast is shifted to the 
right slightly, as skilled guidance such as the HCCA and GDMI are 
more aggressive in depicting Pena's turn to the northwest, but 
overall reflects a middle ground solution between these two models 
and the previous advisory.
 
As it has been stated over the past few days, Pena's intensity 
forecast remains challenging, as the overall environment suggests 
favorable conditions for strengthening, yet Pena has held steady all 
the while. What little data that's available in depicting Pena's 
structure implies that the storm's central vortex lacks structure, 
which has hindered opportunities for intensification. In fact, it is 
entirely possible that Pena currently lacks a closed low-level 
circulation, however this is hard to confirm without a complete 
scatterometer pass. As a result of this uncertainty, Pena's 
intensity forecast has been held flat over the next several days, 
with the possibility that the system could be declared a remnant low 
later today. Should scatterometer data confirm a closed circulation, 
however, Pena could experience at least modest strengthening, given 
its environment of warm SSTs, low vertical wind shear, and ample 
mid-level moisture.
