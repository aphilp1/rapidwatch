Tropical Storm Pena Discussion Number  14
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   CP042026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
500 PM HST Mon Nov 09 2026
 
Pena continues to be poorly organized this afternoon, with a
loosely defined low-level circulation located on the western edge of
the deep convection. Unfortunately, recent scatterometer passes
missed the system, leaving some uncertainty regarding the current
surface wind field. Based on earlier SAR data and a relatively
steady-state satellite appearance, the initial intensity is held at
35 kt for this advisory.
 
The initial motion is estimated to be 275/12 kt. Pena is forecast to
continue moving generally westward, steered by a low- to mid-level
ridge located to its north in the central Pacific. This ridge is
expected to weaken in response to an approaching shortwave trough,
which should result in a more northwestward motion. The updated NHC
track forecast is very similar to the previous advisory and lies
near the HCCA and the Google DeepMind aids.
 
Although environmental conditions appear somewhat favorable with
warm sea surface temperatures and abundant mid-level moisture, Pena
continues to struggle to organize due to persistent mid-level wind
shear, and not being able to establish an inner core. Toward the end
of the forecast period, dry air is anticipated to entrain into the
circulation, which could cause the system to degenerate into a
remnant low sooner than currently indicated. The NHC intensity
forecast lies near the middle of the guidance envelope, and
short-term fluctuations in strength are likely. While it is possible
that Pena could open up into a surface trough due to its fast
forward speed and the aforementioned shear, the official forecast
maintains it as a tropical cyclone through the end of the period.
