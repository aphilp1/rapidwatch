Tropical Storm Pena Discussion Number  15
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   CP042026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 PM HST Mon Nov 09 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that the convective structure associated 
with Pena has changed little over the past several hours. Deep 
convection has remained mostly confined to the northeastern and 
eastern portions of the circulation, due to moderate 
south-southwesterly vertical wind shear. A recent scatterometer pass 
showed a somewhat elongated low-level circulation and a highly 
asymmetric wind field, with the strongest winds located in the 
northeastern quadrant. The latest subjective and objective intensity 
estimates remain in the 30 to 45 kt range. Given these estimates and 
the relatively steady satellite presentation, the initial intensity 
is held at 35 kt.

The initial motion is westward, or 270/12 kt. This general motion is 
expected to continue during the next 48 h as a low- to mid-level 
ridge builds to the north, with the center of Pena forecast to pass 
several hundred n mi south of Johnston Atoll. Thereafter, a gradual 
turn toward the west-northwest is forecast as a weakness develops in 
the ridge in response to a shortwave trough moving across the 
northwestern Pacific. The latest NHC track forecast has been shifted 
southward from the previous prediction toward the latest HCCA and 
GDMI aids.

Moderate south-southwesterly vertical wind shear and possibly some 
drier mid-level air continue to limit Pena's ability to strengthen. 
Although guidance indicates that the shear should decrease somewhat 
during the next 12 to 24 h, only a few intensity models support 
modest strengthening over the next few days. Sea surface 
temperatures near 29 C are expected to persist along the forecast 
track. However, increasingly dry mid-level air beginning around 60 h 
and strengthening southwesterly vertical wind shear toward the end 
of the period should inhibit intensification. Given these factors, 
little overall change in intensity is forecast, although some 
fluctuations in strength are certainly possible. Pena could still 
degenerate into a remnant low or open into a trough, particularly 
later in the forecast period. The latest NHC intensity forecast is 
unchanged from the previous prediction and lies near the middle of 
the guidance envelope.
