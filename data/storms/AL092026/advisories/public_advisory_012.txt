DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 1000 AM CDT (1500 UTC), the center of Hurricane Isaias was
located near latitude 27.7 North, longitude 87.3 West. Isaias is
moving toward the north-northeast near 16 mph (26 km/h). This 
motion is expected to continue through this evening, followed by a 
northward motion on Saturday. On the forecast track, Isaias is
expected to approach the U.S. northern Gulf Coast this afternoon and
make landfall within the warning area this evening.
 
Maximum sustained winds are near 120 mph (195 km/h) with higher 
gusts. Isaias is a category 3 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson 
Hurricane Wind Scale. Fluctuations in intensity are possible through 
this afternoon, but Isaias is expected to be at or near major 
hurricane strength when it makes landfall this evening. Weakening is 
expected beginning tonight after Isaias makes landfall, and the 
storm is likely to become post-tropical by late Saturday.
 
Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 35 miles (55 km) from the
center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 205 miles
(335 km).
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 959 mb (28.32 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Isaias can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header MIATCDAT4 and WMO header WTNT44 KNHC.
 
STORM SURGE: The combination of a dangerous storm surge and the
tide will cause normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by
rising waters moving inland from the shoreline. The water could
reach the following heights above ground somewhere in the indicated
areas if the peak surge occurs at the time of high tide...
 
AL/FL Border to Grayton Beach, FL...6-9 ft
Grayton Beach, FL to Indian Pass, FL...4-7 ft
Indian Pass, FL to Steinhatchee River, FL...4-6 ft
Dauphin Island, AL to AL/FL Border...4-6 ft
Steinhatchee River, FL to Suwannee River, FL...3-5 ft
Mouth of the Mississippi River, LA to Dauphin Island, AL...3-5 ft
Lower Mobile Bay...3-5 ft
Suwannee River, FL to Yankeetown, FL...2-4 ft
Upper Mobile Bay...2-4 ft
 
The deepest water will occur along the immediate coast in areas of
onshore winds, where the surge will be accompanied by large and
dangerous waves. Surge-related flooding depends on the relative
timing of the surge and the tidal cycle, and can vary greatly over
short distances. For information specific to your area, please see
products issued by your local National Weather Service forecast
office.
 
For a complete depiction of areas at risk of storm surge inundation,
please see the National Weather Service Peak Storm Surge Graphic,
available at <a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?peakSurge.'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?peakSurge.</a>
 
WIND: Hurricane conditions are expected within the Hurricane Warning
this evening into early Saturday, with tropical storm conditions
beginning during the next few hours. Hurricane force winds are
expected to spread inland as far as 100 miles from the
coast into southern Alabama. Tropical storm conditions are expected
in the warning areas beginning in the next few hours.
 
RAINFALL:  Through the weekend, Isaias is expected to produce 
rainfall amounts of 4 to 8 inches across southern Alabama, the 
Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and southwest Georgia. Localized 
totals up to 15 inches are possible, particularly within banding 
across the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend. 

Across much of the remainder of the Southeast, rainfall amounts of 2 
to 5 inches are expected.  Localized higher amounts up to 10 inches 
are possible, especially across the Southern Appalachians and 
coastal Carolinas.  

Flash and urban flooding is possible across the region, with 
considerable impacts likely across much of southern Alabama and the 
Florida Panhandle into southwestern Georgia. River flooding is also 
expected, some of which could be significant. 

For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with 
Isaias, please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall 
Graphic available at
<a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf</a> and the Flash Flood Risk 
graphic at <a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.</a>
 
TORNADOES: A tornado risk will begin this afternoon and continue
tonight in coastal areas of Alabama, the Florida Panhandle, and
southern Georgia. The tornado threat will continue Saturday across
parts of eastern Alabama, the Florida Panhandle, Georgia, and the
Carolinas.
 
SURF: Swells from Isaias are affecting the western and northern
portions of the Yucatan Peninsula and are spreading northward toward
the Gulf Coast. These swells are likely to cause life-threatening
surf and rip current conditions. Please consult products from your
local weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: <a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents</a>
