DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 100 AM CDT (0600 UTC), the center of Post-Tropical Cyclone Isaias
was located near latitude 31.5 North, longitude 86.5 West. The
post-tropical cyclone is moving toward the north near 20 mph (31
km/h), and this general motion is expected today. A motion toward 
the north-northeast or northeast is forecast on Sunday. On the 
forecast track, Isaias is expected to move across Alabama and into 
the Tennessee Valley later today and Sunday.
 
Maximum sustained winds have decreased to near 65 mph (100 km/h) 
with higher gusts.  Continued weakening is expected, but Isaias is
expected to continue producing gusty winds well inland before 
weakening below gale force later today.
 
Tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 160 miles (260 km)
from the center.
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 1000 mb (29.53 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Isaias can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header MIATCDAT4 and WMO header WTNT44 KNHC.
 
STORM SURGE: The combination of a dangerous storm surge and the
tide will cause normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by
rising waters moving inland from the shoreline. The water could
reach the following heights above ground somewhere in the indicated
areas if the peak surge occurs at the time of high tide...
 
Santa Rosa/Okaloosa County Line, FL to Suwannee River, FL...2-4 ft
 
The deepest water will occur along the immediate coast in areas of
onshore winds, where the surge will be accompanied by large and
dangerous waves. Surge-related flooding depends on the relative
timing of the surge and the tidal cycle, and can vary greatly over
short distances. For information specific to your area, please see
products issued by your local National Weather Service forecast
office.
 
For a complete depiction of areas at risk of storm surge inundation,
please see the National Weather Service Peak Storm Surge Graphic,
available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?peakSurge.
 
WIND: Tropical storm conditions are ongoing in the tropical storm 
warning area for the next several hours.
 
RAINFALL: Through the weekend, Isaias is expected to produce 
rainfall amounts of 4 to 8 inches across southern Alabama, the 
Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and southwest Georgia. Localized
totals up to 15 inches are possible, particularly within banding
across the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend.
 
Across much of the remainder of the Southeast, rainfall amounts of 2
to 5 inches are expected.  Localized higher amounts up to 10 inches
are possible, especially across the Southern Appalachians and
coastal Carolinas.
 
Flash, urban, and river flooding is possible across the region, with
considerable impacts likely across much of southern Alabama and the
Florida Panhandle into southwestern Georgia. Considerable flash
flooding and landslides are also possible across the Southern
Appalachians.
 
For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with
Isaias, please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall
Graphic available at
hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf and the Flash Flood Risk
graphic at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.
 
TORNADOES: A risk for tornadoes will persist overnight across the
Florida Panhandle and north Florida into southern Georgia. The
tornado threat will continue today across parts of the Florida
Panhandle, Georgia, and the Carolinas.
 
SURF: Swells from Isaias are affecting much of the Gulf coasts of
the United States, Mexico, and western Cuba. These swells are
likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
Please consult products from your local weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents
