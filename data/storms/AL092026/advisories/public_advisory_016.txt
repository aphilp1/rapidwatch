DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 1000 AM CDT (1500 UTC), the center of Post-Tropical Cyclone
Isaias was located near latitude 32.8 North, longitude 86.8 West.
The post-tropical cyclone is moving toward the north-northwest near
10 mph (17 km/h) and this motion is expected to continue today, 
with a turn to the northeast tonight into Sunday.
 
Maximum sustained winds are near 35 mph (55 km/h) with higher 
gusts, especially in areas of elevated terrain. Wind gusts may 
increase during the day today, with weakening speeds expected 
tonight into Sunday.
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 1006 mb (29.71 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Isaias can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header MIATCDAT4 and WMO header WTNT44 KNHC.
 
RAINFALL: Rainfall totals of 3 to 6 inches with localized maximum
amounts of 10 inches are possible across the Southern Appalachians
from northeast Georgia into the western Carolinas and from the upper
coast of South Carolina into eastern North Carolina.   Additional
rainfall totals of 1 to 2 inches possible across the remainder of
the Southeast into northern Florida.
 
Flash, urban, and river flooding is possible across the region, with
considerable impacts, including landslides, possible across the
southern Appalachians. Significant river flooding across the western
Florida Panhandle will crest today through Sunday.
 
For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with
Isaias, please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall
Graphic available at
<a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf</a> and the Flash Flood Risk
graphic at <a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.</a>
 
TORNADOES: A couple of tornadoes are possible today across eastern
Georgia and the Carolinas.
 
WIND: Strong wind gusts continue to pose a threat across the
southeastern United States today, including Georgia, the southern
Appalachians, and extending west through much of central and
northern Alabama, Mississippi, and Tennessee. Scattered power
outages and downed trees and powerlines may occur, with the risk
highest in elevated terrain.
 
SURF: Swells from Isaias will continue to affect much of the
Gulf coasts of the United States, Mexico, and western Cuba this
weekend. These swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf and
rip current conditions. Please consult products from your local
weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: <a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents</a>
