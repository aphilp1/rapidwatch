DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 500 AM AST (0900 UTC), the center of Subtropical Storm Josephine
was located near latitude 38.7 North, longitude 68.3 West. The storm 
is moving toward the northeast near 13 mph (20 km/h), and this 
motion, along with an increase in forward speed, is expected to 
continue into tonight. A gradual turn toward the east is forecast on 
Friday and into the weekend. On the forecast track, Josephine should 
move away from the United States and remain south of Nova Scotia and 
Newfoundland during the next couple of days.
 
Maximum sustained winds are near 65 mph (100 km/h) with higher 
gusts. Some additional strengthening is forecast through the day 
today while Josephine begins to transition to an extratropical 
cyclone. Josephine is expected to become a powerful extratropical 
cyclone with hurricane-force winds by early Friday.
 
Winds of 40 mph extend outward up to 415 miles (665 km) from the
center.
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 988 mb (29.18 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
WIND: Gale-force winds are possible in gusts along portions of the
northeastern United States coast through today. See products issued
by your local National Weather Service office for more details.
 
COASTAL FLOODING: Minor to locally moderate coastal flooding is
ongoing along portions of the U.S. Mid-Atlantic coast and will
continue through the rest of the week around times of high tide.
Please refer to products issued by your local National Weather
Service forecast office for more information.
 
SURF: Large swells generated by Josephine are affecting much of the
eastern seaboard of the United States and portions of Atlantic
Canada. These swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf and
rip current conditions. Please consult products from your local
weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: <a href='https://www.hurricanes.gov/graphics_at5.shtml?ripCurrents'>hurricanes.gov/graphics_at5.shtml?ripCurrents</a>
