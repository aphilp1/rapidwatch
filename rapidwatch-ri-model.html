Could the model have predicted hurricane intensification?

1

Rapid intensification (RI) is an increase of 30 kt (35 mph) or more in a hurricane's maximum winds within 24 hours. It is the hardest behavior in hurricane forecasting to predict, and the most dangerous when it occurs near the coast.

2

We rebuilt the National Hurricane Center's RI guidance — the SHIPS Rapid Intensification Index (SHIPS-RII) — and replayed it on 122 historical Gulf hurricanes, with every forecast out-of-sample: the model never trained on the storm it is forecasting. The result: the model is genuinely better than guessing — but not by much. With no model at all, a forecaster would simply quote the historical average every time: a 6.6% chance of RI. The rebuilt model's forecasts are 9.4% more accurate than that baseline across the Atlantic, but only 5.7% better on Gulf storms — and some of the Gulf's fastest intensifications drew probabilities below 4%, warnings that never came.

Methodology

Probabilities come from a SHIPS-RII-class statistical model trained on the SHIPS developmental dataset (RAMMB/CIRA) with HURDAT2 best tracks. Every number is a true out-of-sample hindcast: forecasts for each storm come from a model run that never saw that storm (leave-one-storm-out), scored on 8,806 forecasts across 538 Atlantic storms, 1982–2023.
